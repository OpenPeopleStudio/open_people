## Bring your own chips (BYOC)

The proposal calls this "bring your own compute" (BYOC). It is a form of colocation.

- Open People would build and run the powered, cooled, secured shell: grid connection and power, cooling, network, physical and cyber security, and 24/7 operations. (CONFIRMED as proposed)
- Tenants would supply and own their chips and servers. (CONFIRMED as proposed)
- Possible tenants are AI labs, hyperscalers, chipmakers and companies with their own silicon. These are target kinds only. None has been approached. None has agreed to anything. Do not name specific companies as possible tenants. (CONFIRMED)
- Why BYOC: chips are the biggest share of AI data-centre cost. GPUs alone are about 39% of AI data-centre capital. (REPORTED, Bernstein) GPUs and custom AI accelerators are about one-third of all data-centre capex. (REPORTED, Dell'Oro)
- In Open People's model, chips are 54% to 76% of the capital for a 100 MW campus. (ESTIMATE)
- At 100 MW, owner-operated would need about C$3.9 billion of chips plus C$1.25 to C$3.28 billion of facility. Under BYOC, Open People would raise the facility only. (ESTIMATE)
- Put another way, the capital Open People must raise would fall from about C$5.1–7.2 billion to C$1.25–3.28 billion at 100 MW. (ESTIMATE)
- The trade-off: BYOC captures a smaller share of value. About C$291 of each MWh in colocation fees under BYOC, against about C$4,047 of GPU time per MWh owner-operated before costs, or about C$502 net per MWh fully loaded in the base case. (ESTIMATE) BYOC would need roughly 46% to 24% of the capital. (ESTIMATE)
- How tenants would pay: a fee per kilowatt of contracted IT capacity per month (kW-month), plus energy passed through at cost. (CONFIRMED as the proposed model)
- Price benchmark: wholesale colocation in primary North American markets averaged US$196.25 per kW-month in H2 2025 (CBRE). (REPORTED) That is C$275.87 at 1.4057 CAD per USD. (ESTIMATE, conversion)
- Rates for a new remote market like Labrador: UNKNOWN.
- A hybrid is likely: BYOC halls for tenants, plus a small owner-operated block for Open People's own sovereign inference service and the research campus. (ESTIMATE, the proposal's view)
- Which model to use (BYOC, owner-operated or hybrid) is decided by Gate 7 in the study. (CONFIRMED as planned)
- A cold climate may allow free cooling with outside air. Labrador's climate data are not yet analysed. (UNKNOWN)

## The value of AI inference per unit of power (the value model)

- One MWh of power can run about 430 GPU-hours on NVIDIA B200 chips, at a facility efficiency (PUE) of 1.30. At a cloud price, that is about C$4,047 of GPU time, before costs. (ESTIMATE)
- Fully loaded (chips, building, financing, staff, energy), the base case nets about C$502 per MWh. The low case is a loss. (ESTIMATE)
- Energy is a small share of the value: about 3% of GPU-hour revenue at the Market Block rate. (ESTIMATE)
- Owner-operated compute lives or dies on utilization, GPU prices and the cost of capital. That is the case for BYOC and for signing customers before buying hardware. (ESTIMATE, the proposal's conclusion)
- This is a model, not a forecast. The study would replace it with an NL-specific model. (CONFIRMED as stated)

## 25-year view (2027 to 2051)

- Chips use less energy per token every year. (REPORTED history; projections ESTIMATE)
- Energy's share of inference cost rises: about 2% today at NL rates, about 10% by 2051 in the base case. (ESTIMATE)
- Against Virginia, NL saves about C$56 per MWh today and about C$91 per MWh in 2051 (nominal). (ESTIMATE)
- Power, not price per token, is the binding limit. Whoever holds firm, clean megawatts holds the scarce input. (ESTIMATE, the proposal's conclusion)
- The main long-run risk: if demand stalls, the same output needs fewer megawatts. The model cannot settle this. (ESTIMATE)

### Five study workstreams

- A. Power and grid: supply available, firm or non-firm, when, at what price, through what interconnection process.
- B. Site and engineering: where; cost to build and run a pilot node, a first tranche and a full campus; chip plan; BYOC shell specs.

- D. Secure, sovereign inference: a security design, from site to model. Design only; no certifications yet.
- E. Partners and engagement: Labrador communities; Indigenous governments and organizations (no contact to date; partnership, if any, on terms each nation sets); project structure with NL Hydro and capital providers; research-campus partners; the 20% public-mission compute reserve.

### Later phases (only if the study says go)

- Phase 1b: pre-development to FEED-ready (engineering, interconnection studies, environmental assessment, Indigenous engagement capacity, legal, site control).
- FEED: front-end engineering before a final investment decision.
- Phase 2: a pilot node, one inference server.
- Phase 3: a staged campus, up to 500 MW.
- Phase 4: the research campus.

## The seven go or no-go gates

Scored pass or fail at month 8. Gates 1 to 6 must all pass for a go. Gate 7 picks the operating model. (CONFIRMED as planned)

1. Power and rate path: NL Hydro or the province confirms in writing a credible path to supply, with timing, and a route to a rate.
2. Transmission: an identified interconnection route for the shortlisted site.
3. Indigenous consent path: at least one Indigenous government or organization in Labrador chooses to continue conversations, on terms it sets. Consent is not presumed. If no nation wishes to continue, or a nation objects, the gate fails.
4. Customer interest: at least one credible target customer signs a non-binding expression of interest.
5. Capital path: named capital providers and federal programs, with at least one willing to review Phase 1b.
6. Secure, sovereign design: the reference architecture is complete.
7. Operating model: whether BYOC has tenant interest; if not, owner-operated only, if gates 1 to 6 pass.
