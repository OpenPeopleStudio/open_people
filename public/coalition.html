Coalition partner brief · August 2026

Newfoundland and Labrador exports its best asset raw. We're building the buyer.

The province generated roughly 43 TWh of renewable electricity in 2023 and ships most of it out of province under contracts written for a different century. The August 17, 2026 Churchill Falls agreement — not yet binding — reopened the only question that matters for the next decade: do those electrons fund someone else's grid, or Labrador's industrial future? Phase 2 is our campaign to make AI compute a serious answer — and to make sure the value stays here.

Churchill Falls is among the largest hydroelectric plants in North America — second in Canada only to Robert-Bourassa. It produces roughly 34–35 TWh a year. Roughly 90% of it has been sold to Hydro-Québec under a 1969-lineage contract running to 2041, at a historical base export price on the order of 0.2¢/kWh.

An honest caveat we hold ourselves to

That 0.2¢ figure is the historical Québec export price. It is not a rate available to us, to you, or to any industrial customer in the province today. Newfoundland Power general service runs roughly 11–13¢/kWh delivered. We never claim otherwise, in any room, and we ask partners to hold the same line — the credibility of this campaign depends on it.

The gap is the point. Media-reported cumulative profit from Churchill Falls to 2019 runs on the order of $28B to Québec against roughly $2B to Newfoundland and Labrador. At modern wholesale values, energy at Churchill Falls scale would be worth billions annually — a figure we use only to illustrate the size of the value gap, never as a forecast.

This is not an anti-Québec campaign. It is a value-retained-in-province campaign, and it needs Québec as a functioning counterparty.

The pricing detail most people get wrong

The rejected MOU's headline "5.9¢/kWh average" was not a flat rate. Per the IRC technical briefing it decomposed into roughly 3.8¢ from 2024–2041, then roughly 16.7¢ from 2042–2075. NL Hydro has now said Hydro-Québec will pay 1.8¢/kWh beginning in 2027, escalating about 14% a year to 11.5¢ by 2041. Québec's proposed data-centre tariff remains 13¢. Partners who model the old MOU path, or who treat the new export price as an industrial rate, will get the offtake case wrong.

The IRC recommended new transmission from Churchill Falls to Labrador West specifically to enable energy-intensive industry. The August 17 agreement funds that line with $1 billion (2026 NPV) in federal support. The corridor now has money. Compute still has to earn peer status alongside critical minerals — which remain first in line.

03 — The buyer

AI infrastructure has become a firm-power buyer of last resort.

Global data-centre electricity demand is projected to roughly double — the IEA puts it at 415 TWh in 2024 rising to 945 TWh by 2030. AI training campuses want firm, high-load-factor, carbon-free power at tens to hundreds of megawatts per site, and they increasingly cannot get it.

Cold climate helps, but it is a tiebreaker, not the moat — liquid cooling has eroded much of the PUE advantage. Firm clean power at scale is the moat. That is what Labrador has and almost nowhere else in eastern North America does.

Scale discipline: 1 GW of continuous load consumes roughly 8.76 TWh a year. So the province's entire renewable output is on the order of a handful of large AI campuses. This resource is enormous for Newfoundland and Labrador and finite against global AI ambition. We plan against the finite number.

The sequence

social equity → credibility + doors → government relations + offtake assembly + coalition → bankable demand package (+ optional 1–10 MW micro-node) → Crown / Innu / infra partners build Labrador capacity → Open People = anchor tenant + sovereignty services (recurring) → LabradorCo = developer promote / carried stake

Two vehicles, on purpose

Open People is the operating company: anchor tenant, demand aggregator, software layer. LabradorCo — a separate development vehicle, incorporation pending — is the convener: it assembles the coalition, site options and federal packages, and earns developer promote. It plays the Aker role from the Stargate Norway structure.

The split exists so that Crown, Innu and infrastructure partners have a clean legal surface to take equity in, without entangling the operating company. It also means our incentives on the facility are promote and carry, not construction margin.

The micro-node floor

A 1–10 MW facility inside the existing recall block is the financeable floor — small enough to build without hyperscale capital, large enough to be real. It does two jobs: it proves the product, and it kills the ghost of Great North Data, the crypto operation that went bankrupt in 2019 owing NL Hydro and ACOA, and which still shapes how "data centre" is heard in this province. A working, permitted, non-crypto facility answers that better than any deck.

08 — Where you fit

What a coalition partner actually contributes.

We are not raising from you in this conversation. The coalition is assembled before the capital is, deliberately — a partner list is what makes the federal package and the provincial ask credible in the first place.

Indigenous partner
Equity from day one, not consultation at the end. Federal sovereign-compute programs require Indigenous participation; the IRC requires it; the project genuinely requires it. We want this conversation with the commercial arm first, structured as partnership, and we take direction on sequencing from you rather than assuming it.
Utility / telco / infrastructure developer
You build and own the steel. We bring the demand and the political path. What we need from you now: an honest read on whether a Labrador node is buildable on your terms, and what would have to be true for a term sheet in Y2. What you get: an anchor tenant, a federal co-funding path, and a provincial narrative already in motion.
Connectivity / subsea
The single hardest open question. The Labrador backbone is thin — roughly a 48-fibre route from 2012 — and Bell scrapped the Labrador North broadband build in 2025 after a 340% overrun. A Goose Bay landing, dark fibre access, or a credible route-diversity answer changes the project's risk profile more than any other input.
Engineering / remote-industrial operators
Close our cost gaps. Sixteen of thirty-eight capital lines are flagged as unresearched — camp, FIFO premium, winter logistics, insurance, cold-climate envelope, substation. Real operating numbers from anyone who has built in Labrador are worth more to us right now than capital.
Institutional and public-sector demand
Tell us what residency-bound compute you would actually buy. Bankable anchor offtake is what unlocks project finance. A non-binding letter describing volume, term and data-residency requirements is the most valuable single document in this whole campaign.
Advisors and connectors
Warm introductions, in the right order. Our sequencing principle: information before advocacy, partners before politicians, never an ask before the brief has been read. An introduction that respects that order is worth ten that don't.
09 — How we work

The rules we'll hold ourselves to, and ask you to hold too.

  • Evidence before narrative. Every public claim maps to an audited validity pack. If it isn't in there, we don't say it.
  • No crypto. Explicit, permanent, and in every public material. The Great North Data precedent is why "data centre" is a loaded phrase in this province, and we are not repeating it.
  • Never lead with "cheap power." We lead with bankable demand, jobs, sovereignty and federal co-investment. The arbitrage framing loses this argument and deserves to.
  • Compute alongside minerals, not against them. Critical minerals are first in line and we are not trying to displace them. Shared transmission, shared community benefits.
  • Indigenous engagement is first-class. Not a consultation footnote appended to a finished plan.
  • Hospitality for the coalition, not for officials. Partners, engineers, capital and advisors are welcome at our table. Ministers and public officials get a briefing in their office. This is a compliance line under the provincial Conflict of Interest Act and the federal Lobbyists' Code, and we don't blur it.
  • Inversion before expansion. Every major bet is stress-tested against written failure stories with kill-switch metrics before it gets resources.

What is not proven — our standing honesty list

We would rather you hear this from us. It is not proven that the province will allocate firm Labrador power to AI over mining. It is not proven that fibre economics support training-cluster connectivity at scale. It is not proven that public-sector offtake alone is large enough to bank a facility. It is not proven that Open People survives disintermediation once large partners arrive — that depends on owning contracts and software. It is not proven that the August 17 agreement will be signed into binding form on the announced timeline, or that it will free near-term megawatts for a compute node. It is not proven that an Indigenous partnership path exists for any specific site — that is site-specific work, not a generic assumption, and it is one of the reasons we are having this conversation before we have a site. Our legal position that in-province energy use with data export faces no energy-law ban is a research opinion that counsel has not yet validated. And we do not yet have the current Labrador industrial rate schedule in hand.

Anyone who tells you these are settled is selling something. We're telling you they're the work.

If the value of Labrador's electrons is going to be decided before March 2027, we'd rather be in the room with a coalition than write about it afterward.
