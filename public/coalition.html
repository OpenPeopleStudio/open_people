Sources: Canada Energy Regulator (2023 data, modified 2026-03-25); NL Hydro generation assets.

Churchill Falls is among the largest hydroelectric plants in North America — second in Canada only to Robert-Bourassa. It produces roughly 34–35 TWh a year. Roughly 90% of it has been sold to Hydro-Québec under a 1969-lineage contract running to 2041, at a historical base export price on the order of 0.2¢/kWh.

An honest caveat we hold ourselves to

That 0.2¢ figure is the historical Québec export price. It is not a rate available to us, to you, or to any industrial customer in the province today. Newfoundland Power general service runs roughly 11–13¢/kWh delivered. We never claim otherwise, in any room, and we ask partners to hold the same line — the credibility of this campaign depends on it.

The gap is the point. Media-reported cumulative profit from Churchill Falls to 2019 runs on the order of $28B to Québec against roughly $2B to Newfoundland and Labrador. At modern wholesale values, energy at Churchill Falls scale would be worth billions annually — a figure we use only to illustrate the size of the value gap, never as a forecast.

This is not an anti-Québec campaign. It is a value-retained-in-province campaign, and it needs Québec as a functioning counterparty.

The pricing detail most people get wrong

The rejected MOU's headline "5.9¢/kWh average" was not a flat rate. Per the IRC technical briefing it decomposed into roughly 3.8¢ from 2024–2041, then roughly 16.7¢ from 2042–2075. NL Hydro has now said Hydro-Québec will pay 1.8¢/kWh beginning in 2027, escalating about 14% a year to 11.5¢ by 2041. Québec's proposed data-centre tariff remains 13¢. Partners who model the old MOU path, or who treat the new export price as an industrial rate, will get the offtake case wrong.

The IRC recommended new transmission from Churchill Falls to Labrador West specifically to enable energy-intensive industry. The August 17 agreement funds that line with $1 billion (2026 NPV) in federal support. The corridor now has money. Mines remain first in line. Compute still has to earn peer status on the merits — it is not reserved.

03 — Who the power is for

Mines and Labrador industry first. Compute only if the paper writes it.

The coalition is assembled around keeping firm power in Newfoundland and Labrador — for operating mines, towns, Indigenous partners, and other industrial load that lives here. It is not assembled around an Open People offtake, a reserved Labrador data-centre block, or a FID path we do not have.

Compute is optional context for leftover firm hydro — not a queue position, and not why this coalition exists. Detail: openpeople.ai/compute. No firm MW, price, or policy preference for Labrador compute is confirmed.

Cold climate helps, but it is a tiebreaker, not the moat. Firm clean power at scale is the scarce asset. Scale discipline: 1 GW of continuous load consumes roughly 8.76 TWh a year. The province's entire renewable output is on the order of a handful of large campuses. This resource is enormous for Newfoundland and Labrador and finite against global ambition. Plan against the finite number — and against mining's first claim on it.

The sequence we will not skip

public voice + contract transparency → in-province optionality in writing (retain, recall, unused-default) → Innu partnership before large Labrador builds → mines / towns served first → leftover firm power eligible for named uses (incl. compute if written) → partners own any steel; Open People stays catalyst

Two vehicles, still hypothetical

Open People is the operating company and the public voice: government relations, evidence, and — only if partners build capacity — a software layer. It is not an anchor tenant today and it is not a demand aggregator with contracts in hand.

LabradorCo — a separate development vehicle, incorporation pending — would be a clean legal surface for Crown, Innu, and infrastructure partners to take equity in, without entangling the operating company. It is not formed, has no offtake, and has no reserved megawatts. If it is ever used, incentives on a facility would be promote and carry, not construction margin.

A financeable floor — if compute is written eligible

A 1–10 MW facility inside existing recall headroom is the planning floor partners sometimes ask about — small enough to discuss without hyperscale capital, large enough to be real. It is not permitted, not reserved, and not FID. One job it would do, if it ever existed, is kill the ghost of Great North Data, the crypto operation that went bankrupt in 2019 owing NL Hydro and ACOA, and which still shapes how "data centre" is heard in this province. No crypto. Permanent.

16 open gaps we will not paper over

Of 38 cost lines, 16 are flagged as research gaps — the honest ones for remote Labrador. FIFO and labour premium versus metro. Workforce camp. Heavy logistics, port and winter-road risk. Insurance adders. Site prep and cold-climate envelope. On-site substation. Upstream transmission, if assigned. Submarine capacity share. Indigenous agreements budget. A partner with operating history in any of these closes a gap the moment they join. That is a real and immediate form of contribution.

08 — Where you fit

A coalition to keep the power here — not to join a campus we do not have.

We are not raising from you in this conversation. The coalition is mines, towns, Indigenous partners, infrastructure, and whoever would actually use in-province power. A partner list makes the provincial ask credible. It is not a substitute for offtake Open People does not hold.

Indigenous partner
Equity from day one, not consultation at the end. Nothing large in Labrador proceeds without Innu Nation. Partnership, royalty, and Gull Island tariff path are unresolved post-vote. We want the conversation with the commercial arm first, structured as partnership, and we take direction on sequencing from you rather than assuming it. We do not speak for Innu Nation.
Mining / resources / towns
You are first in line. What we need from you now: an honest read on whether unused retain would actually serve Labrador load, or slide west by default. What you get: a public campaign that does not try to jump the queue with a reserved compute block.
Utility / telco / infrastructure developer
You would build and own any steel. We do not bring bankable offtake. What we need from you now: an honest read on whether a Labrador industrial or optional compute node is buildable on your terms if the contracts write eligibility. What you get: a public campaign for in-province optionality already in motion — not an anchor tenant we pretend to be.
Connectivity / subsea
The single hardest open question for any compute path. The Labrador backbone is thin — roughly a 48-fibre route from 2012 — and Bell scrapped the Labrador North broadband build in 2025 after a 340% overrun. A Goose Bay landing, dark fibre access, or a credible route-diversity answer changes the risk profile more than any other input. Mining loads need the same corridor honesty.
Engineering / remote-industrial operators
Close our cost gaps. Sixteen of thirty-eight capital lines on a hypothetical node are flagged as unresearched — camp, FIFO premium, winter logistics, insurance, cold-climate envelope, substation. Real operating numbers from anyone who has built in Labrador are worth more right now than capital.
Institutional and public-sector demand
Tell us what in-province load you would actually use. Mining, town, institutional, or residency-bound compute if the province writes it eligible. A non-binding note describing volume and term is useful. It is not an offtake Open People holds, and it does not bank a facility on its own.
Advisors and connectors
Warm introductions, in the right order. Sequencing: information before advocacy, partners before politicians, never an ask before the brief has been read. An introduction that respects that order is worth ten that don't.
09 — How we work

The rules we'll hold ourselves to, and ask you to hold too.

  • Evidence before narrative. Every public claim maps to an audited validity pack. If it isn't in there, we don't say it.
  • No crypto. Explicit, permanent, and in every public material. The Great North Data precedent is why "data centre" is a loaded phrase in this province, and we are not repeating it.
  • Never lead with "cheap power." We lead with in-province use, jobs, and optionality in writing. The arbitrage framing loses this argument and deserves to.
  • Compute alongside minerals, not against them. Critical minerals are first in line and we are not trying to displace them. Shared transmission, shared community benefits.
  • Indigenous engagement is first-class. Not a consultation footnote appended to a finished plan.
  • Hospitality for the coalition, not for officials. Partners, engineers, capital and advisors are welcome at our table. Ministers and public officials get a briefing in their office. This is a compliance line under the provincial Conflict of Interest Act and the federal Lobbyists' Code, and we don't blur it.
  • Inversion before expansion. Every major bet is stress-tested against written failure stories with kill-switch metrics before it gets resources.

What is not proven — our standing honesty list

We would rather you hear this from us. It is not proven that the province will allocate firm Labrador power to AI over mining. It is not proven that fibre economics support training-cluster connectivity at scale. It is not proven that public-sector load alone is large enough to bank a facility. It is not proven that Open People survives disintermediation once large partners arrive. Open People does not hold offtake, a FID path, or a reserved Labrador DC block — anyone who reads this brief as a campus build plan has misread it. It is not proven that the August 17 DCIA will be signed into binding form on the announced timeline, or that it will free near-term megawatts for any industrial node. House endorsement is not that signature. It is not proven that an Indigenous partnership path exists for any specific site — that is site-specific work, not a generic assumption, and Innu Nation's post-vote position is unresolved. The Wind SPE under DCIA §13 is unnamed; federal Canada (not CPP) up to 40% SPE equity is described; a ~$8B / 100% CPP-held “A” company remains unknown. Our legal position that in-province energy use with data export faces no energy-law ban is a research opinion that counsel has not yet validated. And we do not yet have the current Labrador industrial rate schedule in hand. No firm MW, price, or queue for Labrador compute is confirmed.

Anyone who tells you these are settled is selling something. We're telling you they're the work.

If the next generation's firm power is going to be priced while this paper is still paper, we'd rather a coalition were in it than writing about it afterward.
